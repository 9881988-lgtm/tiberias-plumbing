Event budget

The hidden costs of bringing your own caterer

Outside catering can protect menu choice and budget, but the food quote is not the total. Compare venues with one complete event-cost worksheet.

Structor Robotics editorial team14 August 20266 min read
In this guide1. Build the full cost stack2. Price the building, not just the room3. Compare the same service level

Build a complete outside-catering budget that includes venue fees, staffing, rentals, kitchen access, bar, insurance and cleanup.

What to remember

  • Add venue and caterer costs in one sheet.
  • Price staffing and rentals by service style.
  • Confirm kitchen and power before choosing equipment.
  • Keep a contingency for contract changes.

1. Build the full cost stack

Start with venue rental and catering, then add outside-vendor fees, insurance, permits, security, tables, linen, china, warming equipment, delivery, staff meals and gratuity.

Separate fixed costs from per-person costs. That makes it possible to compare a higher-rent flexible venue with a lower-rent venue that adds more operational charges.

2. Price the building, not just the room

Ask about load-in time, elevator access, power, refrigeration, sinks, prep surfaces and waste removal. A prep kitchen is not necessarily a cooking kitchen.

If the caterer needs a tent, generator, portable refrigeration or extra transport, those costs can erase the apparent saving of an open policy.

3. Compare the same service level

A drop-off buffet, staffed buffet and plated dinner require different labor and rentals. Ensure every quote includes the same guest count, service hours, menu, beverage plan and cleanup.

Record tax and mandatory service charges separately from optional gratuity. Ask what changes if the final guest count moves up or down.

The one number to compare

Total event cost = venue + food + labor + rentals + logistics + bar + compliance + cleanup + tax/service + contingency.
