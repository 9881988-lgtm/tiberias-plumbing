Venue policy checklist

How to verify an outside-catering policy before touring a venue

‘Outside food allowed’ can mean anything from one licensed caterer to a true open-vendor policy. Verify the operational details before spending time on a tour.

Structor Robotics editorial team14 August 20267 min read
In this guide1. Name the exact catering need2. Ask five operational questions3. Turn the answer into a contract item

A venue-screening script for couples who need their own caterer, cultural menu, religious practice or a lower food budget.

What to remember

  • Ask who may prepare, deliver and serve food.
  • Separate permission from fees and insurance.
  • Confirm kitchen, bar and cleanup rules.
  • Get the final policy in the contract.

1. Name the exact catering need

Tell the venue whether you need a professional caterer, a culturally specific team, family-prepared food or only dessert from outside. Those are different requests and may trigger different health, insurance and staffing rules.

Include guest count, service style and whether food arrives ready to serve. A precise request is easier to answer than ‘Can we bring our own food?’

2. Ask five operational questions

Who must be licensed and insured? Is there an outside-catering fee? What kitchen or staging space is included? Who handles alcohol? Who is responsible for trash, grease and final cleanup?

Ask whether a preferred-vendor list is optional or mandatory. If an exception is possible, identify who approves it and when approval becomes binding.

3. Turn the answer into a contract item

A sales email is useful for screening, but the signed agreement controls the event. Ask the venue to state the approved caterer, all related fees, access hours, equipment and cleanup obligations.

Reconfirm before paying a non-refundable catering deposit. Policies and availability can change, and the venue and caterer need compatible timelines.

Copy this opening question

We plan to use an independently licensed caterer for [menu/service]. Is that allowed for [guest count], and can you list every required fee, insurance document, kitchen restriction and bar rule?

Policy language decoded

PhraseWhat it may meanFollow-up
Outside food allowedLimited items or deliveryWho may serve it?
Preferred caterersList may be optionalIs off-list approval available?
Approved caterers onlyClosed or conditional listHow is approval obtained?
Self-cateringMay be venue-specificWhich kitchens and events qualify?

Tour only after the policy fits

A beautiful venue cannot fix a catering rule that blocks the menu. Confirm the policy first, then compare aesthetics, dates and total price.
